## 1. Visual Identity Statement

**One-line rule:** *Soft, rounded, bright cartoon heroes in a world whose patterns, clothing and objects are unmistakably Vietnamese, readable in one glance on a landscape phone.*

This builds on the concept's anchor and adds one condition: everything is judged on a low-end phone held sideways. The style is a modern kids' cartoon. Vietnamese identity comes from motifs, materials and period objects. We do not imitate a traditional painting style. This is Luan's choice over the Đông Hồ and lacquer styles.

### Principle 1: Friendly heroes, respectful heroes
Characters are rounded, warm and expressive. They are never comic-silly, never mocked, and never made to clown for a laugh. A child should want to *be* the hero, and a teacher should be comfortable showing the hero in class. Warmth comes from round forms and open faces. Respect comes from upright posture, calm eyes and period dress that is drawn with care.

*Design test:* when a pose or expression could be read as either cute or silly, **choose the dignified version**. If it would make a teacher or the history reviewer uncomfortable, redraw it.
*Serves:* **Đúng sự thật** (True to history), **Sống lại câu chuyện** (Live the story).

### Principle 2: Motifs as accents, not noise
Bronze-drum rings, lotus, bamboo, rice and period clothing carry the Vietnamese identity. They belong on frames, borders, banners, costume trims and far background layers. They do not belong on gameplay surfaces or touch targets. A motif placed at the edge of a screen says "Vietnam". The same motif placed under a button says "clutter".

*Design test:* when a screen is hard to read at a glance, **remove motif detail first**, starting with the layer nearest the action. Do not shrink the hero, the enemy or the buttons.
*Serves:* **Nhỏ chơi được** (Small hands can do it), **Sống lại câu chuyện**.

### Principle 3: The story is told by the world, never by violence or painted text
Drama lives in the sky, the water, the wind and the banners. Defeat is shown with a knock-back, a "poof", a tumble or a retreat. No text is ever painted into art, because every word must switch between VI and EN. Meaning is carried by shape, icon, colour and voice.

*Design test:* when a beat needs more drama, **turn up the environment** (clouds, waves, wind) and leave the injury out. When an image seems to need a word, **replace it with an icon or a separate localised text layer**.
*Serves:* **An toàn cho trẻ** (Safe for kids), **Học mà vui** (Learn by playing).

## 2. Mood & Atmosphere

Each game state gets its own lighting key, colour temperature and energy level, so a child knows where they are in the loop before reading anything. The table gives the overview. The subsections give the detail.

| State | Key light | Temperature | Saturation | Energy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Story scene | Soft lamp-glow, vignette | Warm | Medium, paper-toned | Low |
| Mission beat | Clear morning sun | Neutral-warm | High | Medium |
| Battle beat | Low sun breaking through storm | Cool world, warm hero | High contrast | High |
| Quiz | Flat, even, shadowless | Neutral | Low background, high answers | Low |
| Victory | Golden hour | Hot-warm | Highest | Peak, then settles |
| Calm ending line | Dawn or dusk haze | Cool-soft with one warm point | Low | Very low |
| Map / timeline | Bright diffuse daylight | Neutral-cool | Medium | Low-medium |

### Story scene (picture + voice)
- **Emotional target:** "Sit close, a story is starting." Curiosity and safety.
- **Lighting:** a warm, soft key that falls off into a gentle vignette, like a picture book under a lamp. No hard shadows.
- **Descriptors:** intimate, warm, hushed, storybook, inviting.
- **Energy:** low. Mostly still illustration with slow parallax drift and small breathing motion.
- **Mood carrier:** **the paper frame.** A warm paper-grain border with a bronze-drum ring motif tells the child that this part is told *to* them before they play it.

### Side-scrolling mission beat
- **Emotional target:** "I'm going on a journey." Adventure with confidence and no threat.
- **Lighting:** clear morning sun from behind the camera, with short soft shadows. The playfield is evenly lit so nothing important hides in shade.
- **Descriptors:** bright, open, breezy, hopeful, readable.
- **Energy:** medium, with a steady forward rhythm.
- **Mood carrier:** **the parallax sky and horizon.** A wide sky band and far layers of river, rice fields or hills ⟨placeholder: location to be sourced⟩ move slowly behind the hero and give a feeling of travel.

### Battle beat
- **Emotional target:** "This is the big moment, and I can do it." Excitement and courage. Never fear.
- **Lighting:** the world turns cool, with a storm-indigo sky and racing clouds, but **gold shafts of sun break through and light the hero**. The hero keeps a warm rim light. Enemies sit in the cooler ambient light.
- **Descriptors:** windswept, dramatic, surging, brave, heroic.
- **Energy:** high. Everything in the environment moves, while the playfield ground stays stable and readable.
- **Mood carrier:** **the wind.** Banners snap, clouds race, water lines and grass all lean in one direction. Drama is never shown with blood, wounds, fire on people or a red sky. A red sky is ruled out because it reads as blood and danger.

### Quiz
- **Emotional target:** "I remember this!" Calm focus and pride in knowing.
- **Lighting:** flat and even with no directional drama. The background is a softened, desaturated still from the mission.
- **Descriptors:** calm, clear, quiet, friendly, uncluttered.
- **Energy:** low. Only the answer pictures and the child's tap animate.
- **Mood carrier:** **the big cream answer cards.** Large picture answers on clean cream panels, with nothing competing behind them. A wrong answer gets a gentle "try again" treatment, never alarm (see §4).

### Mission ending / victory
- **Emotional target:** "We did it, and I helped make history." Joy, pride and celebration.
- **Lighting:** golden-hour warmth floods the scene, with a strong bloom on the hero and the stars.
- **Descriptors:** triumphant, golden, glowing, festive, proud.
- **Energy:** peak for a moment, then settling to a warm hold.
- **Mood carrier:** **the gold stars and the unfurling banner,** with a light fall of lotus petals. Victory is shown as the hero standing tall and the land at peace. It is never shown with defeated enemies on the ground.

### Calm ending line (a hero who died for the country)
- **Emotional target:** "They gave everything for us, and we remember them." Respect, quiet gratitude and gentle sadness without fear.
- **Lighting:** a soft dawn or dusk haze, low-contrast and cool, with **one warm point of light**. No dramatic shadows.
- **Descriptors:** still, gentle, reverent, tender, peaceful.
- **Energy:** very low. Near-stillness, slow drift and a long hold.
- **Mood carrier:** **a single warm light that stays.** Examples are a lantern on the river, the hero's banner resting in still air, or the hero's place on the map glowing on. There is no death scene, no fallen body and no weapon pointed at the hero. *How and whether to tell each hero's ending is a content decision for the content owner and Luan. This section only sets the visual treatment once they decide.*

### Map / timeline menu
- **Emotional target:** "This is my country, and I'm filling it with heroes." Belonging, curiosity and collecting.
- **Lighting:** bright, diffuse daylight with no single key, like a map laid on a sunny table.
- **Descriptors:** welcoming, orderly, hopeful, expansive, home.
- **Energy:** low-medium. Lit places pulse gently and the next hero beckons.
- **Mood carrier:** **lit places on the map of Vietnam.** Each completed hero glows bronze-gold on a soft green-and-river-blue land, and the timeline strip below fills in the same way. *Map borders and coastline must come from an authoritative source; to be sourced.*

## 3. Shape Language

The grammar: **circles mean friendly and safe, upward triangles mean the hero and courage, wide bottom-heavy ovals mean slow and harmless, and long horizontals mean calm land.** Sharp, jagged, downward-pointing shapes are kept out of anything a child must look at closely.

### Hero silhouette
- Built from **circles with an upward accent**: a round head, a compact rounded body and one upward-pointing element as the signature. This could be a crest, a topknot, a raised banner or spear tip, or a flowing sash ⟨placeholder: Trần-era headwear, armour and weapon to be sourced⟩.
- **The signature element must survive a solid-black fill at thumbnail size.** If you fill the hero black and shrink it to about 48 px tall, a child should still recognise *this* hero.
- The hero faces right, toward the direction of travel, with an open, forward-leaning stance. Attack poses extend outward and upward. Defend poses become a compact round shield shape.
- **Historical banner text.** Trần Quốc Toản is traditionally associated with a banner bearing a slogan ⟨placeholder: to be sourced⟩. Per the no-text rule, the banner is drawn as a **plain shape and colour**. Any words appear as a separate localised layer or through voice. Painted characters are never used.

### Enemy silhouettes (soft and non-scary for 6-year-olds)
- **Bean, pear and dumpling shapes**, wide at the bottom and narrow at the top. They look heavy and slow, which matches their forgiving behaviour.
- **No sharp points that face the player.** Helmets are rounded domes, weapons are blunt and oversized like props, and there are no teeth, claws, spikes or narrowed glaring eyes. Eyes are small round dots or simple curves. Expressions run from mild grumpiness to surprise.
- They face left, toward the hero. **They are never taller than the hero.** A "big" enemy grows wider, not taller, so it never looms over the child's avatar.
- **Defeat is a shape change, never injury.** The enemy squashes, its hat or helmet pops off, it tumbles back, it vanishes in a round "poof" cloud, or it runs off. Every defeat state is readable as "that enemy is out of the way" without any harm being shown.
- **No ethnic caricature.** The enemies represent an invading army ⟨placeholder: Yuan-era armour and dress to be sourced⟩. They are identified by armour shape, colour family and banner. Facial features are never exaggerated. This needs the content owner's and Luan's sign-off before any enemy concept is final.
- **Thumbnail test:** in solid black at phone size, hero and enemy differ in at least two of these: **upward vs. downward-heavy mass, signature accent, facing direction, size**.

### Environment geometry
- **Long, calm horizontals:** rice terraces, river banks and the ground plane. The walkable ground is always one clear, continuous band with a bold top edge, so a child always knows where to stand.
- **Vertical rhythm:** bamboo groves and posts break up the horizontals at regular intervals and help pace the scroll.
- **Gentle curves for built forms:** upswept roof eaves, gates and boats ⟨placeholder: Trần-era architecture and boat forms to be sourced⟩.
- **Edge hierarchy:** playable surfaces have the boldest outlines, mid layers have soft outlines, and far layers have none. Only things the child interacts with carry hard edges.
- **Battle variant:** the same geometry tilts. Wave crests curl, clouds stretch diagonally and grass leans. The ground band stays level.

### UI shape grammar
- **Everything is rounded.** Panels and cards are large-radius rounded rectangles, and buttons are pill shapes. There are no sharp corners anywhere in the UI.
- **Action buttons (move, attack, defend, special) are circles,** the largest round objects on screen. They sit in the bottom-left (move) and bottom-right (actions) thumb zones in landscape. Each has a **unique icon and a unique outer shape cue**, so buttons never depend on colour alone (see §4). *Minimum touch-target size is set with ux-designer in the accessibility spec. Visually, the targets must read as big, chunky and pressable.*
- **Bronze-drum ring** is the signature frame motif for hero portraits, map places and star rewards. It is a circle with a concentric pattern band, used on frames, never as a full pattern inside a touch area. **Exception (Luan's rule 4, 2026-11-14):** an in-mission pad may carry **one** motif accent, e.g. a thin drum ring on the defend pad's rim, never under its icon.
- **Stars** are soft five-point stars with rounded tips: a filled shape when earned and an outline shape when not.

### Hero shapes vs. supporting shapes
- **The hero has the most complex silhouette and is the only figure with a full-saturation warm accent.** The eye always finds the hero first.
- **Supporting characters** (villagers, elders, the king's court ⟨placeholder: to be sourced⟩) use simpler silhouettes, fewer costume details and lower saturation, with calm, rounded, mostly static forms.
- **Props and pickups** use the simplest shapes of all, a single readable icon-like form with a soft bounce or glow when interactive.
- Hierarchy rule: **Hero > interactive objects > enemies > supporting characters > environment.** When two elements compete, simplify the lower one.

## 4. Color System

### Primary palette
Natural, earthy pigments anchor the world, with warm accents reserved for meaning. Outlines use a warm ink brown instead of black, which keeps the cartoon soft and nods to natural pigments.

| # | Name | Hex | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **River blue** (Xanh sông) | `#3E8FC4` | Water, calm, the land's lifeline. Map rivers, defend actions |
| 2 | **Rice green** (Xanh lúa) | `#7CB342` | Countryside, peace, growth. Safe ground, map land, correct answers |
| 3 | **Bronze gold** (Vàng đồng) | `#E8A93B` | Heroism, reward, heritage (bronze drums). Stars, lit map places, hero accents |
| 4 | **National vermilion** (Đỏ son) | `#D64A2E` | Courage and the nation, **used sparingly**: the hero's banner or sash, key symbolic moments |
| 5 | **Paper cream** (Giấy dó) | `#F3E6C8` | Story, memory, calm. Story frames, UI panels, quiz cards |
| 6 | **Storm indigo** (Chàm bão) | `#3B3F6B` | Battle drama and the turning point. Battle skies, deep water, dusk |
| 7 | **Ink brown** (Mực nâu) | `#4A3428` | Structure. All outlines, icon strokes, UI text |

*Hex values are starting proposals. They will be tuned on real low-end phones and checked against the colour-blind simulations below.*

### Red must never read as blood
- Vermilion appears only as **flat, clean, shaped areas** such as a banner, sash, lacquered prop or seal-shaped icon. It is never a splash, drip, spatter, stain or gradient pooling at the bottom of a shape.
- It never appears **on or near an enemy at the moment of defeat**, never on the ground, and never on water.
- It leans **orange-warm** (vermilion), not dark crimson, and is kept bright and opaque.
- Budget: in gameplay, vermilion should cover only a small fraction of any frame, and the hero should usually carry it. If a screen feels red, remove red.

### Semantic usage
| Meaning | Colour | Always paired with |
|---|---|---|
| This is the hero / courage | Vermilion accent + bronze gold | Signature silhouette, warm rim light |
| Interactive / reward | Bronze gold | Glow pulse, bounce animation |
| Safe / correct / progress | Rice green | Tick icon, a positive sound cue (audio-director owns) |
| Calm / defend / water | River blue | Shield icon |
| Enemy | Desaturated cool slate-violet (proposed `#7A7395`) | Bottom-heavy silhouette, left-facing |
| Drama / turning point | Storm indigo | Wind motion, cloud movement |
| Story / memory | Paper cream | Paper-grain frame |

### Per-area temperature
- **River:** cool and fresh, with blue-greens and morning-bright highlights. Water carries the most saturated blue on screen. Reflections stay simple (bands, not detail) for readability and performance.
- **Citadel** ⟨placeholder: Trần-era capital to be sourced⟩: warm, with ochre walls, terracotta roofs and bronze-gold detail. It feels orderly and dignified, and is the warmest non-battle area.
- **Countryside:** warm-neutral, with rice greens, straw gold and soft sky. It is the "home" palette and the closest to the map's colours.
- **Battle:** the world shifts cool (storm indigo sky, dark river) while **the hero stays warm**. That temperature split is what makes the hero pop and keeps the moment brave rather than frightening. No red or orange skies.

### UI palette (diverges from the world)
The world is natural, textured and layered. The UI is **flat, high-saturation, thickly outlined and untextured**, so buttons never blend into backgrounds. This applies Gestalt figure–ground: UI is always "figure".

| Role | Hex | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Panel fill | `#FFF6E3` | Lighter and cleaner than the world's paper cream |
| Outline / text | `#3A2A20` | Thick stroke on every UI element |
| Attack button | `#F07F1E` | Tangerine, kept clearly apart from the world's vermilion |
| Defend button | `#2E7FD0` | Saturated blue |
| Special button | `#FFC531` | Star gold with glow, only when available |
| Move pad | `#FFF6E3` + ink arrows | Neutral, so the action buttons lead |
| Correct | `#3FA34D` | Always with a tick icon |
| Try again | `#9C8FB8` | Soft lavender-grey with a circular-arrow icon. **Not red**, so a wrong answer never feels like shame |
| Star earned / unearned | `#FFC531` / outline only | Fill vs. outline shape |

### Colour-blind safety
No information is carried by colour alone. Every pair that could fail has a non-colour backup.

| Pair at risk | Fails for | Backup |
|---|---|---|
| Vermilion vs. rice green (hero sash on fields; red/green in general) | Protanopia, deuteranopia | Hero identified by silhouette and rim light; the sash is not needed to find the hero |
| Correct (green) vs. try again | Protanopia, deuteranopia | Tick vs. circular-arrow icon, card shape animation (pop vs. gentle wobble), distinct sound cues (audio-director owns) |
| Attack (tangerine) vs. special (gold) | Protan/deutan (similar warm hues) | Different icons, different outer shapes (attack: round; special: star-edged ring), special glows only when available |
| River blue vs. rice green (map land vs. water) | Tritanopia | Water has animated ripple lines, land has a bold coastline outline |
| Star gold vs. paper cream (stars on panels) | Tritanopia, low vision | Ink-brown outline on stars, filled vs. outline shape for earned and unearned |
| Hero vs. enemy (warm vs. cool) | All types, in low light | Silhouette contrast and facing direction (§3), which is the primary identifier |

*Verification:* every key screen is checked in greyscale and in protan, deutan and tritan simulation before it is approved. Screenshots go in `production/qa/evidence/`.

## 5. Character Design Direction

**The player character is a child-sized hero, not a superhero.** Trần Quốc Toản (and
each later hero) is drawn at about **3 heads tall** with a large head, big readable eyes,
and a compact, rounded body. That fits the young age the story gives him and lets a 6-year-old
see themself in him. Heroes are **warm and earnest**: brave and determined, never
smirking, goofy or angry at the player. They serve *Live the story* and *Small hands
can do it*.

**Telling characters apart at a glance (phone size, landscape):**

| Type | Silhouette cue | Colour cue | Behaviour cue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero | Tallest of the cast at the front, one strong prop (Quốc Toản: the banner and a short spear or staff ⟨placeholder: weapon to be sourced⟩) | Brightest, warmest outfit on screen, with a gold accent | Always faces the direction of travel |
| Followers / allies | Smaller and rounder than the hero; they share one outfit colour with him | The hero's team colour, desaturated | Walk behind the hero, cheer |
| Enemies (invaders) | Wider and boxier helmets and shapes, so they read as "other" rather than scary ⟨placeholder: Mông–Nguyên armour to be sourced⟩ | Cool, muted colours, never red | Slow wind-up pose before every attack: a telegraph a child can read |
| Neutral NPCs (villagers, guards) | Soft, everyday shapes with no weapon raised | Natural earth tones | Idle and friendly; the Bình Than guards raise a hand, not a weapon |

**Animation set per hero** (side view, facing right; mirrored for left):
`idle`, `run`, `attack` (one staff swing of 0.28 s; the hit lands at 45%, so wind-up and
follow-through are short — combat GDD `attack_time`, `attack_hit_at`), `defend` (shield or guard
pose, held), `special` (hold pose for hold-meter; banner raise for training), `hit` (a small
knock-back hop and a blink, **no injury shown**), `cheer` (end of beat). Enemies face **left**
and use the combat GDD's state names: `walk` (enter/approach), `windup`, `strike`, `rest`, `hurt`
(stumble, helmet wobble), and `flee` (drops the weapon, leaves the screen waving its arms).
**There is no defeated or lying-down pose for anyone.**

**Expression and pose:** exaggerated, squash-and-stretch poses that read in silhouette.
Faces show determination, surprise and joy. Anger (beat 2, the orange) is shown with
puffed cheeks and clenched fists, **never** tears of rage or a scary face. A teacher must
be comfortable pausing on any frame.

**Detail at camera distance:** characters fill about 20–25% of the screen height in
landscape. Detail that doesn't survive at that size is cut: there's no fine embroidery, and
period clothing is suggested by **2–3 shapes** (headwear, collar or sash, sleeve shape). Clothing
and hair of the Trần era are ⟨placeholder: to be sourced from the reference picture books
(§ 9, ref 2) and Luan's sign-off⟩.

## 6. Environment Design Language

**Setting:** 13th-century Đại Việt under the Trần dynasty, in riverbanks, wharves, villages, rice
fields, a family home and courtyard, and river battle sites. The architecture uses timber
frames, curved tiled roofs, bamboo fences and wooden boats ⟨placeholder: period details
to be sourced; no specific building is claimed as historical⟩. The world reads as
**Vietnamese first**: bamboo, banana leaves, lotus ponds and wide rivers.

**Texture philosophy: flat painted shapes with soft gradients, no photo texture and no
PBR.** Flat shapes stay readable on low-end phones, keep the cartoon warm, and let motifs
(bronze-drum rings, lotus) sit as clean graphic accents.

**Parallax layers (landscape side-scroller):**

| Layer | Content | Detail | Contrast |
|---|---|---|---|
| Far sky | Sky colour, sun or moon, cloud bands | Lowest | Lowest |
| Far | Mountains, a distant citadel silhouette | Low | Low |
| Mid | Trees, roofs, river, boats | Medium | Medium |
| Play | The ground the hero runs on, and obstacles | Clear edges | **Highest** |
| Foreground (optional) | Grass and leaves passing in front | Low, partly transparent | Must never hide the hero or an enemy |

**Prop density:** **sparse on the play layer**, so the space around the hero and enemies is
always clear; richer in the mid and far layers. In story stills and the map, motifs may
be denser. In action beats, motif detail is the first thing removed when a screen gets busy.

**Telling the story without text:** each beat's place is clear from shape alone. Beat 1
is a busy wharf with boats. Beat 2 is a closed gate with a royal canopy. Beat 3 is a home
courtyard with straw dummies and a banner pole. Beat 4 is an open riverbank under a
windy, dramatic sky. Battle drama comes from **wind, waves, clouds and banners**, never
from damage, fire on people, or bodies.

## 7. UI/HUD Visual Direction

### Art view

- **Screen-space HUD**, not diegetic: children need fixed, predictable buttons.
- The **frames, panels and buttons** carry the world's motifs: rounded panels with a
  thin bronze-drum ring border, and a lotus or bird icon on headers. The play area itself
  stays clear.
- **Typography:** one rounded, friendly sans-serif with **full Vietnamese diacritic
  support**, tested on stacked marks such as *ệ, ỗ, ữ, ặ* ⟨placeholder: font to be chosen;
  the licence must allow app embedding⟩. Use two weights (regular, bold) and three sizes
  (title, body, button).
- **Icons:** filled, rounded and illustrated, matching the characters; no thin outline icons.
- **UI animation:** bouncy and short (0.15–0.3 s), with stars that pop and buttons that
  squash when pressed. Nothing flashes.
- **Player text voice** (D-17): see `design/narrative/writing-guide.md`. Vietnamese uses no pronoun
  for the child where possible, and "em" when one is needed.
- **No text in art** (concept rule): every word, including the banner's six words and quiz
  answers, is a live VI/EN text layer over the art.

### UX view

- **Landscape thumb zones:** move left/right sits bottom-left; attack, defend and special
  sit bottom-right. The buttons are big: at least about 12 mm across on a 5.5″ phone ⟨target
  to confirm in the prototype⟩, spaced so a small thumb doesn't hit two at once.
- **Every button has an icon and a distinct shape**, not just colour: attack is round
  (fist or spear icon), defend is a shield shape, special is a star-edged ring (banner icon), as in § 3. Nothing relies on colour
  alone (§ 4).
- **Text size:** body text no smaller than about 18 pt equivalent on a phone ⟨to confirm⟩.
  Every story line has voice, so reading is never required to play.
- **Layout fits both languages:** EN runs longer than VI, so text boxes are sized for EN plus
  20% and never shrink the font to fit.
- **Feedback:** every press gives a visual squash, a sound, and (in a beat) a visible
  result on the character.
- **Conflict to flag:** motif-rich frames versus thumb readability. **Resolution proposed:**
  motifs stay on panel borders and menus; in-mission buttons are plain shapes with a single
  motif accent. **Luan decides** if he wants richer in-mission buttons.

## 8. Asset Standards

### Art view

- **Formats:** source files in a layered format ⟨tool to be chosen⟩. Exports are PNG with
  alpha for sprites and UI, and PNG or WebP for backgrounds ⟨per engine⟩.
- **Naming:** `[category]_[name]_[variant]_[frame].[ext]`, lowercase ASCII with no
  diacritics in file names. For example `char_tranquoctoan_run_01.png`,
  `enemy_soldier_knockback_03.png`, `bg_binhthan_far.png`, `ui_btn_defend.png`.
- **Authoring resolution:** paint at 2× the target (backgrounds 3840×2160 for 1920×1080
  landscape) and export 1× and 2× tiers.
- **Characters:** sprite sheets or cut-out (skeletal) parts, whichever the engine handles
  better ⟨decided after `/setup-engine`⟩. Each hero keeps one consistent proportion
  sheet (turnaround, expressions, the animation list in § 5).
- **No text layers baked into exports.** Text is applied by the game.

### Technical view

- **No engine is set yet** (`project.yaml` has no `engine` block), and **no performance
  budgets are set** (technical-preferences: draw calls, memory ceiling "[TO BE CONFIGURED]").
  So there is no texture memory budget, no atlas size limit and no draw-call target, and
  none is invented here.
- **Known constraints from the concept:** it must run offline on a **low-end Android
  phone**, and every hero adds art to the app size because content ships in app updates (no
  download). Both push toward texture atlases, the 1× tier on low-end devices, and reuse
  of enemy and background sets between heroes.
- **Trade-off to decide after `/setup-engine`:** the ideal is 2× crisp art on every
  device; the constrained option is 1× atlases on low-end Android, where art looks softer
  on high-DPI screens. **Not resolved here.** Revisit with the technical-director once the
  engine and budgets are set.

## 9. Reference Direction

**Decided (Luan, 2026-11-14):** two references are kept, and both are Vietnamese sources.

### 1. Đông Hồ folk prints and Đông Sơn bronze-drum patterns: *for the motif library and pigment palette*
- **Take:** the source library of motifs (bronze-drum concentric rings, sun star and bird bands, lotus, rice and village imagery) for frames, borders, UI rings and costume trim, plus the earthy natural-pigment palette (warm paper ground, ochre, deep natural reds and greens) that informs our primaries. *Every motif's origin and meaning to be sourced and reviewed before use.*
- **Avoid:** copying the woodblock print style itself (flat registration, carved outline look, period figure drawing). Luan chose modern cartoon over Đông Hồ. Also avoid using sacred or ceremonial patterns as decoration without the content owner's review.

### 2. Vietnamese historical picture books for children (e.g. NXB Kim Đồng's *Tranh truyện lịch sử Việt Nam*): *for period objects as Vietnamese kids know them*
- **Take:** how Trần-era clothing, armour, weapons, boats and court settings ⟨placeholder: to be sourced⟩ are presented to Vietnamese children today, so our hero matches the image they already have from school and home. It is a cross-check for recognisability. It is not a historical source, and every detail still goes through the content owner.
- **Avoid:** their realistic battle violence (wounds, bodies, weapons striking people) and their realistic rendering and anatomy. We take what is *depicted*, not *how* it is depicted.
